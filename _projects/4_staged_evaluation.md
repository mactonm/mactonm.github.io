Randomized controlled trials are expensive, slow, and ethically demanding. Running one on a clinical AI system that has not yet earned the right to be tested is a waste of the resources health systems can least afford to lose. This project asks what evidence should precede a trial, and how that evidence should be staged.

## Motivation

Most evaluation frameworks for clinical AI were written with well-resourced hospitals in mind: reliable electronic records, stable infrastructure, and clinical teams with time to supervise a silent deployment. Low-resource health systems rarely match those assumptions, yet they are where AI decision support is most aggressively marketed.

## Approach

We adapt a staged evaluation pipeline: retrospective external validation, silent prospective deployment, human-factors evaluation, and finally a pragmatic trial. At each stage we define stopping criteria that are feasible to measure in settings with sparse data and limited clinical oversight.

## Status

Framework drafted; pilot validation underway with partner sites.
